Person-to-Person Representation in the EUDI Wallet

Prototype plan — acting on behalf of a child, an elderly relative or another adult

Prepared by Martin Woodward · martin@woodwardweb.com · 19 November 2026 · Draft for discussion

1. Why this matters

eIDAS 2 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1183) explicitly covers “a natural person representing another natural person”, and Annex VI lists powers and mandates to represent as attributes Member States must make verifiable. The ARF has a design paper (Topic I, v0.4) but no attestation rulebook exists yet — the official catalogue holds only the PID and mobile driving licence rulebooks. National wallets are due by 24 Dec 2026, so representation will arrive after launch.

Today, delegation lives in national portals, not wallets: Estonia’s Pääsuke (central mandate registry on eesti.ee, queried live over X-Road, open-ended mandates), Latvia’s portal authorisations (limited), and Australia’s per-agency myGov nominees. The new EU Protection of Adults Regulation (adopted by the Council 19 Nov 2026) adds an EU certificate of representation — a natural future input.

2. Goal & requirements

Build an end-to-end prototype where an agent holds a Representation Attestation in their own wallet and a relying party always knows it is dealing with a representative.

3. Architecture

Principal wallet ──(PID via OpenID4VP: grant / revoke)──▶ Representation Attestation Provider (RAP) • mandate registry (Pääsuke-like) Agent wallet ◀──(OpenID4VCI + PID during issuance)──────▶ • mock population register │ • Token Status List └──(OpenID4VP / DC API: agent PID + representation)──▶ Demo relying party (health / social care)
  • Wallet: fork of Paradym Wallet (Apache-2.0; Expo/React Native; Credo). It already supports PID presentation during issuance, DCQL credential sets, ETSI TS 119 602 trusted lists, SD-JWT VC type metadata and transaction data — the hardest building blocks.
  • RAP and RP: Node services on Credo (@credo-ts/openid4vc) — same stack as the wallet.
  • Trust: local trusted list naming the RAP and the demo RP.

4. The credential

  • representation_type: voluntary mandate · power of attorney · guardian of minor · guardian of adult
  • legal_basis: consent · population register · court order (later: EU certificate)
  • principal: name, birth date, pseudonymous identifier
  • scope: domain + actions, e.g. health: read_records
  • representative: claims matching the agent’s PID, plus wallet key binding
Two validity periods.
Mandate validity = the legal authority; may be open-ended (as Pääsuke allows: ValidityPeriod.through = null means indefinite).
Credential expiry = technical lifetime of the wallet copy; short (e.g. ≤ 30 days, a prototype choice) and silently refreshed while the mandate is active. This approximates Pääsuke’s live lookup while still working offline.

5. Key flows

FlowSummary
F1 Grant (elderly relative)Principal logs into RAP portal with wallet, chooses agent, scope and dates, confirms with a signed transaction; agent receives an invite.
F2 AcceptAgent opens invite → OpenID4VCI; RAP requests agent’s PID during issuance, checks match, issues key-bound attestation.
F3 Parent / guardian of minorA legal event (birth, adoption, guardianship or care order) updates authoritative sources; guardian collects one attestation per child with their PID. Valid to 18 unless restricted or revoked (see §6).
F4 Act at a serviceRP requests PID or PID + representation. Wallet shows “Acting on behalf of …” with a picker if several. RP checks trust, signatures, status, binding and scope.
F5 RevokePrincipal withdraws, agent waives, or authority revokes (register/court) → status list updated; refresh stops. Mirrors Pääsuke’s DELETE_WITHDRAW / DELETE_WAIVE.

6. Child authorisations — options (Ireland as worked example)

Key constraint: parentage ≠ guardianship. The GRO registers births, marriages and adoptions, but an unmarried father on the birth certificate is not automatically a guardian. He becomes one through 12 months’ cohabitation incl. 3 with the child (CFRA 2015 s.49), a joint statutory declaration, or a court order — none of which is centrally registered. Under a care order (Child Care Act 1991 s.18) Tusla can consent to medical treatment and passports; parents remain guardians but their powers are restricted in practice.

OptionHow it worksTrade-offs
A. GRO issues at life eventOn birth registration, marriage or Adopted Children Register entry, GRO issues legal_guardian_minor to each guardian, valid to 18. A+: statutory declarations lodged with GRO.Simplest; covers most children from day one. Misses court orders and care.
B. New guardianship registerOne register combining GRO, Courts Service, declarations and Tusla, feeding a single issuer (Estonia/Latvia-style).One clear answer; needs new law and data-sharing.
C. Each authority issuesGRO, Courts Service and Tusla each issue their own credential; the RP reconciles them.No new register; RPs must resolve conflicts; revocation split across issuers.
D. Hybrid (recommended)GRO baseline (A+) plus Courts/Tusla overlays: issue to Tusla, social workers, foster carers or court guardians, and narrow a parent’s scope via a shared restriction/status service.Incremental and legally accurate; needs an agreed restriction model.
E. Migrants & foreign-born childrenIreland has no population register. Immigration registration starts only at 16, EU citizens don’t register, and children born abroad are never in the GRO. Add an evidence route: another member state’s guardianship attestation, apostilled foreign certificates or orders verified by a caseworker (provisional → confirmed), and DSP PPS / Child Benefit links as supporting evidence only. Model it with the OpenID DA Framework.Covers people the registers miss; lower assurance until verified; depends on recognition under the Hague 1996 Convention or EU law.
Adopt from the OpenID eKYC & IDA DA Framework (draft): guardian-dependent relationship type; trigger-based expiry (expires_on_event: age 18, order ends); transfer with continuity (successor_rel_jti) so placement changes leave no protection gap; precedence among concurrent holders; graduated authority by age band; and its Privacy-Preserving Profile (the RP learns “authorised”, not care status, number of children or legal basis). An early, AI-assisted draft, JWT-based — use as a design reference, not a spec.
  • Scope tiers: guardian_full, day_to_day_care, medical_consent, passport_consent, education. Foster carers typically get day-to-day; s.43A orders can extend it.
  • Lifecycles: emergency (8 days) and interim care orders map to short mandates; voluntary care leaves parents in charge; discharge restores scope; all end at 18.
  • Safeguarding: a restricted parent must never learn the placement, school or address — “minimal disclosure” mode for principal claims.
  • Child’s voice: from 16, the child could see who holds authority over them. Prototype: mock GRO, Courts, Tusla and foreign-evidence feeds; Phase 6 grows to 2 weeks.

7. Wallet changes (Paradym fork, behind a feature flag)

  • Distinct representation card and detail screen (principal, type, scope, validity, status)
  • “Acting on behalf of …” presentation banner and a “who are you acting for?” picker
  • Revocation/expiry display and silent re-issuance of short-lived copies
  • Activity log entries (“Acted for X at Y”); “Authorisations I’ve given” (v1 portal link, v2 native)

9. Open questions for discussion

  • Binding: key binding + PID match vs. Wallet Unit Attestation binding — the ARF does not yet specify.
  • Consent evidence: is a wallet-signed transaction enough for a power of attorney, or is a QES required nationally?
  • Principal privacy: what principal data RPs really need; sector-specific pseudonyms?
  • Older minors: should a 15+ child with their own wallet see or approve a parent’s attestation?
  • Authority sources: plug in Pääsuke (Estonia; mandates are informative, not constitutive — see its legal framework) and national registers; cross-border recognition via the Protection of Adults Regulation.
  • Engineering: Paradym tracks alpha Credo builds — pin versions; rebrand per Apache-2.0 obligations.
